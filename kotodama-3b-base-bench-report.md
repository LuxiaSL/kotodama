## Comparability notes by model family

**Pythia** is unusually transparent: the family card fixes the **300B-token** training budget, and the public eval dumps expose the model revision and `num_fewshot: 0`, which makes those rows highly valuable as **true zero-shot base-model references**. The main caveat is coverage: in the retrieved dumps, some of your target tasks were simply absent from the accessible JSON for the checkpoints I inspected. citeturn0view0turn10view0turn19view3

**GPT-Neo and GPT-2 XL** are useful historical floors, but their HF card is weaker on protocol detail than Pythia: it says the scores come from **Eleuther’s evaluation harness**, yet it does not explicitly pin shot count or clearly distinguish `acc` from `acc_norm` for each row in the retrieved text. I would treat those rows as good historical orientation, but not as precise harness-matched comparisons. citeturn29view0

**Cerebras-GPT-2.7B** is a very important anchor because it is a **2.7B compute-optimal baseline** with only **53B tokens**. It is explicitly evaluated **zero-shot** with **lm-evaluation-harness**, but many of the reported cells are **raw accuracy**, so the HellaSwag and ARC comparisons to your `acc_norm` values are only directional unless you rerun them under your exact harness settings. citeturn30view0

**RedPajama / OpenLLaMA / BTLM / StableLM** together map the post-Pythia 3B landscape well, but they are not all protocol-identical. OpenLLaMA uses **lm-eval-harness** with a tokenizer caveat but does not pin shots; BTLM explicitly says **zero-shot** and uses **raw accuracy**; StableLM is the best-documented among them, because its repo publishes either the **harness commit** or the **branch name** and explicitly calls out when metrics are normalized. RedPajama required a mixed-source row because the official card linked benchmark details out to the blog, while ARC/BoolQ coverage came from the BTLM paper’s comparison tables. citeturn36view0turn38view0turn39view5turn42view0turn31view0turn34search1

**TinyLlama** is helpful for token-scaling intuition but is less clean as a harness match. Its `EVAL.md` reports a **GPT4All benchmark table**, says it uses **lm-eval-harness**, and also says it reports **`acc_norm` by default**. That makes HellaSwag and ARC especially informative, but the BoolQ / PIQA / Winogrande numbers should still be treated as a looser comparison than a directly matched lm-eval-harness 0.4.11 run. citeturn25view0turn27search4

**SmolLM2** is the clearest example of a modern small-model recipe outperforming older peers, but it uses **LightEval**, not lm-evaluation-harness. Its row is therefore best read as a **high-quality directional baseline** rather than a strict apples-to-apples harness comparison. The most important protocol mismatch for your table is that the paper reports **aggregate ARC**, not ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge separately. citeturn45view0turn43search7

**Qwen2.5-3B and Gemma-2-2B** were partially screened from official cards, but I did not retrieve source-compatible task tables from those cards before cutoff. ## Synthesis on the params-times-tokens-times-score landscape

The strongest high-confidence conclusion is that your **2.97B / 384B-token** checkpoint is **not token-starved in the obvious sense**. It already sits well beyond compute-optimal short-budget baselines like **Cerebras-GPT-2.7B at 53B tokens**, and it is in the same broad token regime as **Pythia-2.8B at 300B** and **GPT-Neo-2.7B at 420B**. But on the tasks that are most comparable from the published sources, your model does **not** match those Eleuther-era peers: Pythia-2.8B is stronger on **PIQA (73.9 vs 68.4)**, **ARC-Easy (64.4 vs 55.1)**, **ARC-Challenge acc_norm (33.3 vs 27.3)**, **SciQ (88.2 vs 87.0)**, **Winogrande (59.4 vs 55.6)**, and **LAMBADA (64.7 acc / 5.04 ppl vs 49.7 acc / 11.09 ppl)**, while GPT-Neo-2.7B is also ahead on **PIQA, Winogrande, LAMBADA**, and **WikiText perplexity (11.39 vs 17.75)**. That places your current checkpoint **below the trend line** set by the best-documented 300B–420B open 2.7B–2.8B baselines. citeturn19view0turn19view1turn19view2turn19view3turn19view4turn20view0turn29view0

Against the **very short-budget** 2.7B class, the picture is better but still mixed. Relative to **Cerebras-GPT-2.7B**, your model is directionally ahead on HellaSwag and plausibly competitive on ARC-Challenge, but Cerebras remains slightly better on **PIQA** and **ARC-Easy** — and because Cerebras reports **raw accuracy** rather than normalized accuracy on those tasks, even that comparison is only directional. Your own **66B-token Chinchilla-style variant** underperforming your 384B-token main run by a lot is therefore consistent with the public landscape: very short token budgets at this scale are not enough for broad small-model quality, even if the recipe is otherwise compute-optimal. citeturn30view0

Where do your scores “land” in better-fed models? On **HellaSwag** and **PIQA**, they look much closer to a **well-trained ~1B-class model** than to the mature 3B frontier. TinyLlama’s 1.1B model reaches roughly **43.5 / 67.3** by 103B tokens and **49.6 / 69.4** by 503B tokens on HellaSwag / PIQA, which brackets your **46.7 / 68.4** surprisingly well despite the much smaller parameter count and the GPT4All metric caveat. Meanwhile the stronger 3B-class bases — **BTLM-3B-8K**, **OpenLLaMA-3Bv2**, **StableLM-Base-Alpha-3B-v2**, and especially **StableLM-3B-4E1T** — sit far above that zone, usually in the **68–74 Hella range** and **76–79 PIQA range**. citeturn25view0turn39view0turn40view0turn36view0turn42view0

The most conspicuous underperformance is in **language modeling quality** rather than in every knowledge probe equally. Your **WikiText word-perplexity 17.75** is essentially **GPT-2 XL territory (17.48)**, not GPT-Neo-2.7B territory (11.39), and your **LAMBADA 11.09 ppl** is similarly much closer to GPT-2 XL’s **10.63** than to GPT-Neo-2.7B’s **5.63** or Pythia-2.8B’s **5.04**. By contrast, **SciQ 87.0** is only modestly behind Pythia-2.8B’s **88.2**, which suggests the model is **less broken on broad factual / science multiple-choice recall** than it is on the next-token-prediction-heavy capabilities that usually correlate with robust downstream transfer. In plain language: the checkpoint looks **more undertrained in core LM quality than its token count alone would predict**. citeturn29view0turn19view1turn19view3

If I compress that into one judgment: your 384B-token 3B base sits **above very-short-budget compute-optimal baselines, but below the strongest 300B–420B peers and far below the 627B–1.1T+ modern 3B trend**. The public evidence points much more toward **recipe / data / curriculum / optimizer / tokenizer / domain-mixture issues** than toward “we simply needed more tokens” as the primary explanation. citeturn30view0turn29view0turn19view0turn19view1turn36view0turn42view0turn45view0
